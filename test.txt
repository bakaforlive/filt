Grand Theft Auto VI is an upcoming action-adventure game developed and published by Rockstar Games. It is due to be the eighth main Grand Theft Auto game, following Grand Theft Auto V (2013), and the sixteenth entry overall. Set within the fictional US state of Leonida, based on Florida, the story follows the romantic criminal duo of Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos. Players will be able to freely roam Leonida's open world, which features the Miami-inspired Vice City.

Following years of speculation and anticipation, Rockstar confirmed in February 2022 that the game was in development. That September, footage from unfinished versions was leaked in what journalists described as one of the biggest leaks in the history of the video game industry. Grand Theft Auto VI was formally revealed in December 2023. Following two delays, it is scheduled to be released on 19 November 2026 for the PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S.
Setting and characters
Grand Theft Auto VI is primarily set in Vice City, a fictionalised version of Miami.[1]

Grand Theft Auto VI is a single-player, action-adventure game set within an open world.[2] Its fictional US state of Leonida, based on Florida, includes Vice City,[a] Grassrivers, and the Leonida Keys (inspired by Miami, the Everglades, and the Florida Keys, respectively), as well as Ambrosia, Mount Kalaga National Park, and Port Gellhorn.[1] The game world parodies 2020s American culture, with satirical depictions of social media and influencer culture, modern law enforcement tactics and technology such as police body cameras, and references to Internet memes such as Florida Man.[5]

The story follows a criminal couple: Jason Duval, who worked for local drug runners in the Leonida Keys after serving in the Army; and Lucia Caminos, the series's first non-optional female protagonist,[b] who was imprisoned at Leonida Penitentiary after fighting for her family from Liberty City.[8] Following a failed bank heist, the duo encounter a state-wide conspiracy and are forced to protect each other.[9] Other characters include: Cal Hampton, Jason's paranoid friend; Boobie Ike, who runs a business empire in Vice City; Dre'Quan Priest, owner of the record label Only Raw Records with Boobie; Bae-Luxe and Roxy, a musical duo signed to Only Raw under the name Real Dimez; Raul Bautista, a seasoned bank robber; and Brian Heder, a longtime drug runner in the Keys and Jason's landlord.[8]
Development and release

Rockstar Games began preliminary work on Grand Theft Auto VI in 2014 after Grand Theft Auto V's release, with early development starting in late 2018 following Red Dead Redemption 2.[10][11] Principal production began in 2020,[12] code-named Project Americas.[11] Jason Schreier reported the game was "a moderately sized release" that would expand over time to avoid its predecessors' developer crunch,[13] and that it would feature two Bonnie and Clyde–inspired protagonists, including a Latina,[11] and "a significant online mode" akin to Grand Theft Auto Online.[10] He claimed the developers were cautiously subverting the series's trend of joking about marginalised groups.[11][14] In 2018, The Know reported that the game would be set primarily in Vice City and partly in South America,[15] and in 2021, Tom Henderson claimed its map could evolve akin to Fortnite.[16] The game is expected to use the Rockstar Advanced Game Engine.[17] It is the first main Grand Theft Auto game since the 1997 original (and the first overall since 2004's Advance) not to be written by Dan Houser, who left Rockstar in 2020.[18]

The game became highly anticipated in the years before its announcement,[19] and some fans became frustrated by Rockstar's silence, particularly after it announced a re-release of Grand Theft Auto V in 2020.[20] An individual received media attention for interrupting several live stage and television shows to reference the game.[21] Rockstar confirmed development was "well underway" on 4 February 2022,[22] and in July announced Red Dead Online would not receive more major updates as development resources were withdrawn to focus on the upcoming game.[23] Industry sources stated Rockstar reallocated additional resources after planned remasters of Grand Theft Auto IV (2008) and Red Dead Redemption (2010) were paused due to the backlash received by Grand Theft Auto: The Trilogy – The Definitive Edition (2021).[24] Rumours and analysts suggested the budget surpassed US$1–2 billion, making it the most expensive game ever developed,[25] though the figure remains unverified.[26] Some industry figures hoped the game would cost more than the standard $70 to prompt a wider trend,[27] but analysts noted it could limit sales;[28] it was ultimately priced at $79.99.[29]

Grand Theft Auto VI won anticipation awards at the Game Awards and Golden Joystick Awards in both 2024 and 2025,[30][31] and scammers used the anticipation to promote malware and fraud through false promises of early access.[32] A meme indicated bemusement that surprising events occurred "before GTA 6".[33] To avoid competition, several publishers awaited a release date announcement before scheduling or delaying their games;[34] Schreier called it "a massive game of 4D chess playing out across the entire video-game industry".[35] Circana predicted the game would "rebound" the market with record consumer spending;[36] Niko Partners estimated first-day sell-through exceeding $1.2 billion[37] and DFC Intelligence projected first-year earnings of $3.2 billion (40 million copies)—doubling Grand Theft Auto V's highest-grossing launch—including $1 billion in preorders.[38]

Rockstar requested employees cease remote work and return to offices from April 2024 "for productivity and security" in "the final stages of development".[39] The Independent Workers' Union of Great Britain (IWGB) criticised Rockstar for contradicting their promise to maintain flexible working conditions; some employees were concerned it could negatively affect staff health and morale and prompt resignations and crunch,[40] especially as company leaders could continue working flexibly while regular workers could not.[41] Kotaku's Zack Zwiezen reported the decision was partly to avoid a delay,[42] though other journalists and Rockstar's parent company, Take-Two Interactive, said the game remained on schedule for late 2025.[43] The amount of remaining work led employees to expect a delay;[44] in May 2025, the game was delayed to 26 May 2026.[45]

Rockstar fired 34 employees[c] on 30 October 2025, citing public discussion and distribution of confidential information.[46][47][48] The IWGB accused the company of union busting, stating the employees were attempting to unionise with labour organisers;[48] they protested outside Rockstar North and Take-Two's offices.[49] The following week, Rockstar delayed the game to 19 November 2026, citing additional polish.[50] Journalists noted the firings did not cause the delay but could lead to missed deadlines;[51] a Rockstar North employee said morale was "at rock bottom",[52] and Take-Two's stock price briefly dropped by almost 10%.[53] Other developers supported the delay[54] and the Polish politician Witold Tumanowicz jokingly referenced it in a Sejm session;[55] some players expressed their waning interest but others found it acceptable.[56] Analysts expected publishers would reschedule their games to take Grand Theft Auto VI's previous release window or move away from its new date to avoid competition.[57][d]
